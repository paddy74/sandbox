Purpose: the organization, the Business Leader's pain, what each audience cares about, the discovery questions, and the assumptions to state in the first 10 minutes. Facts, thresholds and hero objects come from files 02 and 03.

All organization details below are a **fictional composite for the demo**. Nothing here describes a real command, system, schema or statistic. Every demo number is synthetic. Measured numbers are in the table at the end of this file (synthetic, from the pipeline notebook run).

## 1. The organization (fictional composite)

| Item                | Framing                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who                 | A mid-sized analytic organization: a team of analysts producing assessments from many collection sources.                                                                                                        |
| What they produce   | Assessments and briefs that depend on a shared picture of **objects** (facilities, equipment, events). The demo covers facility and equipment; events are out of scope (file 03).                                |
| Who feeds them      | Algorithms (automated detections) and single-source analysts, each producing observations that overlap in space and time.                                                                                        |
| What they own       | An authoritative object management system (mocked as Postgres), collection and tasking records (mocked as MySQL), observation feeds from SaaS/API platforms (mocked as JSON), and years of finished report text. |
| The Business Leader | Senior leader accountable for analytic timelines and the credibility of the brief (for example a division chief or a chief of analysis). Not technical. Cares about the brief being right and on time.           |
| Already covered     | Company and platform overviews. Do not repeat them.                                                                                                                                                              |

Public-facts rule: any statement about BFO or CCO adoption must be verified against a public source before it is said out loud (file 02, section 5). Nothing in this brief depends on such a claim. If asked, say "I would confirm the exact wording with your ontology team".

**The one-sentence story for the leader:** "Today your analysts spend their time deciding whether observations are the same object; the system makes that call for the obvious cases, queues the hard ones with the evidence attached, and turns the leftovers nobody could use into new candidate objects, and every analyst decision makes it better."

**Why it matters (consequence framing):** inaccurate or incomplete object data has visible real-world consequences. Say this once and plainly; do not dramatize it or add operational detail.

## 4. Discovery questions (leading, open, force specifics)

Ask 3 or 4 of these in the first 10 minutes. Each is open, asks for a number or a named person or a process, and cannot be answered yes or no. Pick based on what the leader volunteers.

| #   | Question                                                                                                                                               | What it forces                                             | A good answer lets me say                                                                                 | A surprising answer changes                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | "Walk me through the last brief where the object count was disputed. How long did it take to settle, and how many people's hours went into it?"        | A specific story, hours of rework, number of people        | "So the cost is roughly [their hours] per brief times how many briefs" (feeds the ROI formula on slide 4) | If the answer is "rarely disputed", lead with recovered observations (Hero C) instead of the count debate (Hero B)                                                |
| 2   | "When an algorithm and an analyst report the same thing slightly differently, how does that get resolved today, and who has the final say?"            | The adjudication process and the decision owner            | "The analyst stays the decider; the system handles the obvious cases and queues the rest"                 | If there is no single adjudicator or no record of decisions, the retraining story needs a different label source (adjudication history is a day-one ask, slide 5) |
| 3   | "What happens to an observation that isn't complete or confident enough to stand on its own? Where does it go, and does anyone ever look at it again?" | The discard policy and volume                              | "Those are the observations the system tries to recover by corroboration"                                 | If they say "we keep everything and review weekly", reframe recovery as time saved, not data saved                                                                |
| 4   | "Where does an object's authoritative record live, and when two systems disagree, what does an analyst have to do to correct it?"                      | System of record, correction workflow, write-back appetite | "The approve/reject action writes back to the object system through a governed MERGE"                     | If the object system is read-only for us, the write-back becomes a proposed-change queue rather than a MERGE                                                      |

Optional fifth, only if the leader is warm and time allows: "What would have to be true in 30 days for you to say this was worth continuing?" (sets up the success criteria on slide 5).

**Do not ask:** "Does my assumption make sense?", "Is that right?", or "Do you have data issues?"

## 5. Assumptions to state up front (first 10 minutes)

State these as a short list ("Here is what I am assuming; tell me where I am wrong"), then run the discovery questions. Each assumption has the answer that would change the demo.

| #   | Assumption stated                                                                                                        | If the answer is different              | Changes the demo how                                                                                                        |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The unit of work is the **Object** (facility, equipment); events come later with the same pattern.                       | Events are the priority                 | Keep the build; say the pipeline carries over using BFO process / CCO Act classes (no event IRIs verified; do not cite any) |
| 2   | Observations arrive from both algorithms and analysts, with overlap in place and time.                                   | Only one producer type                  | Drop the cross-producer corroboration framing; the model feature `is_analyst` is less important                             |
| 3   | Analysts today adjudicate matches by hand and some record of those decisions exists.                                     | No adjudication history                 | Labels must be bootstrapped (rules baseline plus analyst review queue); the model starts as a ranker, not a learner         |
| 4   | The object system is the system of record and can accept governed write-back.                                            | Read-only or a different workflow       | Show approvals as a proposed-change table, not a MERGE                                                                      |
| 5   | Auto-associating above a very high threshold (0.9) is acceptable only if analysts see the uncertain middle (0.5 to 0.9). | The leader wants a human on every match | Set auto-associate to zero and show the system as a prioritization and evidence tool                                        |
| 6   | Data stays in a controlled environment; the demo uses synthetic data only.                                               | The environment is the first question   | Lead with the authorization path as a gating item (slide 5, file 07); do not claim any FedRAMP or IL level                  |
| 7   | Cost matters more than speed of rollout.                                                                                 | Speed matters more                      | Emphasize the 30-day plan and the managed, serverless components                                                            |

Everything about the data is synthetic and says nothing about real-world accuracy. State that once, early, so the leader does not read demo numbers as claims.

## 6. Ten-minute meeting opener flow

| Min          | Move              | Say / do                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00 to 0:01 | Frame             | "I will skip the platform overview. I would like to spend ten minutes making sure I am solving your problem, then show you something built on synthetic data."        |
| 0:01 to 0:03 | State assumptions | Read the 3 to 4 most relevant rows of section 5. "Correct me on any of these."                                                                                        |
| 0:03 to 0:08 | Discovery         | Ask 3 or 4 questions from section 4. Write the leader's numbers on the shared screen or a notepad (hours, people, discard volume). Reflect each back in one sentence. |
| 0:08 to 0:09 | Audience check    | "CDO and engineering colleagues: what would you need to see before this is real to you?" Note each one's answer.                                                      |
| 0:09 to 0:10 | Bridge            | "Based on that, I will show you [Hero B or C, whichever matched]. Everything is synthetic. I will tell you what is mocked and what the production path is."           |

Rules for the opener: no more than 10 minutes; no slides yet; capture at least one number from the leader (it is the seed of the ROI conversation on slide 4); end with a promise to the leader, not a feature list.
